The Orchard at Hollis Lane

The orchard covers four acres behind the packing shed. There are forty
rows of apple trees along the east wall, and eleven rows of pears on
the slope that catches the morning sun. The oldest tree, a russet the
family calls the Bishop, was planted the year the road was paved.

Watering is the question every visitor asks first, and the honest
answer is that it depends on soil the county agent has seen and this
pamphlet has not: when to water is a matter this pamphlet leaves to
the county agent's soil card. What the pamphlet can say is what grows:
the forty rows of apples are split between keepers and cider fruit,
and the pears are all keepers.

Picking starts when the seeds turn brown, never by the calendar. The
crew works the east wall first because it ripens a week ahead of the
slope. Windfalls go to the press the same day they are gathered.
