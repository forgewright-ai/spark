The kettle learns the morning first,
a thin note rising through the house
before the light has found the sill,
before the cat has found the mouse.

I count the stairs I cannot see,
eleven, then the loose twelfth board,
and reach the kitchen by its sound,
the small bright alarm of water poured.

Outside the street is still a rumour.
A bus goes by with no one in it.
I hold the cup with both my hands
and give the day another minute.
