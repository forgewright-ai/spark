# Seven

The ferry was late, which Marguerite had expected, and cold, which she had
not. She stood at the rail with her collar up and watched the harbour
lights smear and steady on the water. Behind her a man was explaining the
tides to a child who had stopped listening some time ago.

She had the letter in her inside pocket. She had read it four times on the
train and once more in the terminal, under the flickering board, and each
time it said the same thing in the same careful hand: come if you can, do
not come if you cannot, either way I will understand. It was the kind of
sentence her brother wrote when he wanted something very much.

The island rose out of the dark the way it always had, a low shape with
one light on it, and she felt the old thing turn over in her chest. Not
dread exactly. Something that had learned to look like patience.

"Nearly there," said the man to the child, who was asleep.
